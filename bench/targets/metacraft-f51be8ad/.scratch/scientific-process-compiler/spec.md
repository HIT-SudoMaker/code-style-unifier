# Scientific process compiler

Status: superseded by ../metalens-sonnet-convergence/spec.md

## Problem Statement

MetaCraft's frozen Rust authority and its Python evidence lifecycle are sound,
but the current Python science was completed as two low-na metalens paths. The
compiler still names metalens-specific data at its center, chooses a proof
through phase-specific branches, and leaves examples to assemble consultation,
admission, sweeping, matching, field evaluation, and conclusion by hand.

That shape can preserve the current propagation-phase and geometric-phase
results, but it cannot remain small when large-na metalenses, holographic
metasurfaces, quasi-bic metasurfaces, and frequency selective surfaces arrive.
Phase, aperture, field propagation, and focus are not universal scientific
stages. Turning each new aim or method into another end-to-end route would
recreate fixed workflows and eventually pressure the frozen Rust core.

The researcher needs one coherent scientific compiler whose public language is
brief, study, and result; whose internal language is claim and method; and whose
execution remains governed by Rust-admitted evidence. It must retain every
successful current propagation/PB scientific and execution contract without
making those contracts the universal architecture.

## Solution

MetaCraft will present one deep, pure scientific compilation seam. It accepts
an immutable brief, reviewable advice, Rust-admitted evidence, and qualified
capabilities, and returns one immutable study. The same inputs always produce
the same canonical study.

Aim and objectives belong to the brief. Route and proof belong to the study.
Inside the compiler, an aim declares terminal claims and registered methods
establish claims from prerequisite claims and evidence under explicit
applicability conditions. The selected claim–method graph is the route; its
complete prerequisite and evidence topology is the proof.

The compiler resolves intent, proves the requested objectives, binds only ready
proof needs to qualified methods and realizations, and closes claims only with
admitted evidence. These are compiler passes, not workflow positions. The
route-neutral runner gathers ready tasks under Rust permits, proposes validated
observations, reads the new authority view, and invokes the same compiler
again.

The system will preserve this mental cadence:

`brief asks → compiler composes → study declares → runner gathers → Rust admits → result answers`

The first executable slice remains the existing single-wavelength low-na
metalens capability. Propagation phase and geometric phase compile through the
same seam and share infrastructure, while their response, polarization,
selection, channel, and acceptance evidence remain scientifically distinct.

## Implementation Decisions

- The Rust authority, its native protocol, and its four Python verbs remain unchanged.
- The primary public scientific Interface is one pure compilation operation that consumes a brief, reviewable advice, admitted evidence, and qualified capabilities and returns one immutable study.
- The public scientific language is brief, study, and result.
- Aim and objectives are brief content. A brief records honest omissions rather than filling unrelated fields with null or sentinel values.
- Design is resolved intent and contains no compiled route, proof, exact solver, or local execution fact.
- Route and proof are study content. Route records the selected claim–method graph and its reasons; proof records the complete prerequisite and evidence topology.
- Claim and method are internal compiler language. A claim is a typed scientific statement under exact conditions. A method establishes one claim from prerequisite claims and evidence under explicit applicability conditions.
- Aim declarations may resolve aim-specific intent, declare terminal claims, and interpret a closed proof. They may not select solver products or own a brief-to-result process.
- Method declarations may state applicability, prerequisites, required capability, produced claim, expected evidence, and pure evaluation. They may not collect briefs, mutate authority, run workers, or return route results.
- The compiler has four internal passes: resolve, prove, bind, and close. These passes are implementation order, not persistent state or public workflow.
- Proof composition is deterministic and independent of registration order. Multiple scientifically distinct applicable proofs remain an explicit unresolved choice unless user fact or deterministic policy selects one.
- The implementation may use a small, private, typed claim–method graph. It will not expose a public rule, graph, registry, plugin, reflection, dynamic-discovery, or AI-planning Interface.
- Advice may rank or explain registered choices but may not invent a claim, method, capability, route identity, binding, qualification, or evidence.
- Capability, method, realization, binding, capacity, and permit remain separate types and meanings.
- A binding is selected per ready proof need and records the exact qualified method, realization, template, material identity, and local facts.
- A task exists only when its prerequisite claims are closed and its binding is complete. It is immutable and carries no mutable status.
- Evidence closes a claim only when its schema, scientific subject, operating conditions, prerequisites, binding, and exact references match the compiled proof.
- Normal incompleteness is returned as typed waiting findings. Broken proof topology, contradictory admitted evidence, invalid bindings, and non-deterministic composition are compilation errors.
- The runner remains route-neutral. It gathers ready tasks under Rust permits, validates observations, proposes receipts, reads the new authority view, and recompiles.
- The existing workstation lane, memory, NUMA, license-capacity, permit, and solver Adapter contracts remain intact.
- Standard briefs move out of the compiler's scientific implementation and become small example inputs.
- The current metalens-specific brief and design center is replaced by aim/objective-aware language without creating one parallel brief lifecycle per aim.
- Propagation phase compiles the golden relationship `focus ← scalar field ← aperture ← phase set ← fixed-height cell library ← periodic complex transmission ← material and height evidence`.
- Propagation phase uses one canonical phase circle on `[0, 2π)`, treats `0 ≡ 2π`, and applies cyclic distance to every phase-matching decision.
- The 8-, 12-, and 16-state propagation proofs and results remain separate.
- Geometric phase compiles the golden relationship `focus ← converted and retained fields ← channel apertures ← analytic orientations ← one anisotropic cell ← Jones response and polarization convention ← material and height evidence`.
- Geometric x- and y-input observations run sequentially under one permit; orientation-specific FDTD tasks are forbidden.
- Propagation and geometric routes may share phase-circle mathematics, aperture contracts, field implementations, runner machinery, authority adaptation, materials, and solver qualification. Their route-specific evidence schemas remain non-substitutable.
- Low-na field completion continues to require the realized aperture, a qualified scalar angular-spectrum implementation, axial search from `0.8f` to `1.2f`, transverse and axial half-maximum bracketing, and the accepted efficiency measures.
- Future large-na, holographic, quasi-bic, and frequency-selective relationships are represented by non-executable golden fixtures during this feature. They validate the language but introduce no speculative solver code.
- Existing successful live Lumerical observations, native construction contracts, height choices, cell libraries, phase sets, apertures, fields, and results remain valid when their exact references satisfy the new compiled claims.
- Old phase-specific compiler branches, route-owned end-to-end conclusion seams, and manual example orchestration are removed only after equivalent behavior passes through the new Interface.
- Module organization follows responsibility rather than lifecycle mirroring: one scientific language Module, one compiler Module, aim declarations, and method declarations. Internal file count is not part of the Interface.
- Naming follows the Sonnet standard: natural nouns, verb operations, paired meanings, no numbered stages, versioned code names, pipeline, workflow, manager, registry, or speculative generic framework.

## Testing Decisions

- The highest and primary scientific test seam is the pure compilation Interface. Tests supply typed briefs, advice, evidence, and capabilities and assert only the resulting study, findings, tasks, route, proof, and result.
- Compiler tests prove canonical determinism: identical exact inputs produce identical study bytes regardless of input ordering where order has no scientific meaning.
- Compiler tests distinguish normal waiting findings from broken invariants and compilation errors.
- Brief tests prove preservation of original wording, declared aim and objectives, constraints, preferences, and omissions, and prove that advice cannot rewrite user facts.
- Proof tests assert observable claim–method relationships rather than private traversal or graph data-structure behavior.
- Propagation golden tests cover height advice and choice, a single fixed-height library, separate 8/12/16 phase sets, cyclic phase distance at the `0/2π` branch, deterministic selection, realized aperture assignment, bracketed field evaluation, and exact result closure.
- Geometric golden tests cover polarization convention, x/y Jones observation under one permit, converted/retained channel derivation, one anisotropic cell choice, analytic orientations, separate channel apertures and fields, bracketed focus evaluation, and exact result closure.
- Cross-route tests prove that propagation response cannot close geometric claims and Jones/channel evidence cannot close propagation claims.
- Evidence tests vary wavelength, polarization, material, height, geometry, route, method, realization, binding, and prerequisite references and prove that mismatched facts are rejected.
- Binding tests prove that proof is formed before product selection and that changing a qualified realization does not change terminal claims.
- Runner integration tests use the existing typed Authority seam and fake/local execution to prove permit acquisition, receipt or close, admitted evidence, recompilation, and crash recovery without durable Python task status.
- Architecture tests keep the private native import confined to the authority Adapter and freeze Rust source and public authority behavior.
- Workstation and product Adapter tests remain at their existing Interfaces; the compiler is not tested through native process placement.
- Live Lumerical tests remain explicitly opt-in and verify only contracts requiring the installed product, license, material read-back, native construction, or native observation.
- Future relationship fixtures compile representative large-na metalens, holographic, quasi-bic, and frequency-selective briefs far enough to assert their terminal claims and absence of unrelated universal stages. They do not execute solvers.
- Large-na fixtures prove that the metalens aim retains focus claims while replacing low-na methods.
- Holographic fixtures prove that reconstruction can close without a focus claim.
- Quasi-bic fixtures prove that resonance and Q claims can exist without phase, aperture, or focus.
- Frequency-selective fixtures prove that scattering and absorption claims can exist without imaging.
- Standard examples are exercised as smoke tests through the public seam; tests do not duplicate their orchestration.
- Replacement tests are written at the new high seam before obsolete route-specific and example-level tests are removed. Tests that inspect implementation details are not layered beneath equivalent Interface tests.
- The full existing default suite must remain green throughout migration. Live external tests are reported separately and are never represented by offline fixtures.

## Out of Scope

- Changing Rust authority source, protocol, schemas, public verbs, native module identity, or lifecycle meanings.
- Implementing large-na vector angular spectrum, Debye/Richards–Wolf evaluation, pointwise recovery, or optimization.
- Implementing holographic synthesis, reconstruction optimization, or fabrication output.
- Implementing quasi-bic eigenmode, radiation-channel, complex-frequency, driven-spectrum, or Q evaluation.
- Implementing frequency-selective scattering, absorption, or angle/polarization sweeps.
- Adding CST, COMSOL, RCWA, eigenmode, or other new product Adapters or solver-native materials.
- Creating a public scientific DSL, graph engine, rule registry, plugin framework, decorator registration, dynamic discovery, or AI planner.
- Replacing the accepted Lumerical template geometry, `grating_s_params` observation contract, mesh accuracy, reference-plane policy, fixed workstation lane, or license-capacity policy.
- Automatically selecting a winner among 8-, 12-, and 16-state results.
- Adding a mutable progress database, retry ledger, task status, workflow position, sequence number, or resume token.
- Redesigning the frozen Rust core to understand aims, claims, methods, routes, solvers, materials, or numerical models.

## Further Notes

This specification implements the accepted decision to compile proofs from
claims and methods and refines the existing evidence-driven study compiler. It
does not invalidate successful scientific observations merely because the
Python organization changes; exact compatible evidence remains reusable.

The implementation should proceed as tracer bullets through the new compiler
seam. The first tracer should compile both current low-na routes without
running a solver. Later tracers should adapt existing propagation and geometric
evidence producers and conclusions, then remove the obsolete paths.

The architecture is judged by its cadence rather than its number of files:

`brief asks → compiler composes → study declares → runner gathers → Rust admits → result answers`
